Platform Brief

One-liner you should be able to say cold:
"Noon DineOut puts your restaurant in front of millions of active noon shoppers — people who aren't even browsing for restaurants yet — and converts them into real diners walking through your door."
🍽️
What is Noon DineOut?
A dine-in discovery and bill-payment platform embedded inside the noon super-app. Restaurants get visibility to noon's existing millions of shoppers, grocery buyers, and daily users — not just people actively looking for restaurants.
🎯
Core Value Prop
Fill slow hours and empty seats with high-intent customers. This isn't a delivery play. It's about footfall, table utilization, and repeat dine-in visits — especially on off-peak slots.
🔮
Where It's Going
Noon is building a full "Going Out" ecosystem — restaurants, salons, spas, events, concerts. Early partners get founding-partner visibility before the platform scales. QR bill-splitting (like Qlub) is in roadmap.
🛡️
Why It's Different
Other platforms rotate the same discount-seekers. Noon's users are shoppers already spending inside the ecosystem — converting them into diners means higher intent and more return visits, not one-time deal chasers.

03

Meeting Flow

1
Warm-Up & Rapport 2–3 min
Compliment the food hall concept and each brand's specific strength. Set the agenda casually: "I'd love to understand your goals for the hall, share how our dineout platform can help fill seats, and explore a 2-month launch structure."
2
Discovery — You Ask, They Talk 10–15 min
This is the most important phase. Ask and listen. The more they talk, the more they commit.
"What are your goals for the first 90 days of this food hall launch?"
"Which days or time slots are hardest to fill?"
"How are you currently generating new diners? What's working, what isn't?"
"How do you feel about discounting? What's your comfort zone on offers?"
→ Mirror back: "So the main thing is filling weekdays without killing margins — did I get that right?"
3
Value Story 5–8 min
Position as a growth partner, not a discount app. Lead with risk reduction:

• Noon already has millions of UAE shoppers — we convert them into diners.
• Pay-for-performance model — you only pay when a real customer redeems.
• 0% lead-gen fee for 60 days — your only "cost" is a controlled offer you design with us.
• "Founding partner" positioning — early movers get priority listing slots & marketing push.
4
Make the Offer 3–5 min
Anchor high, give them levers. Don't say "we want a discount" — say "we're co-investing in launch demand."

Use the offer table from Section 2. Lead with Option B (time-banded) if they mentioned dead hours.
5
Handle Objections As needed
See Section 5 — Objection Playbook. Stay calm, pause before answering, validate before redirecting.
6
Close 2–3 min
Use a direct but collaborative close. Don't leave without a specific next step — even if they don't sign on the spot.
04

The 60-Second Pitch

🎯 Opening — Their Pain
"With the food hall launching, your biggest win is getting consistent footfall — especially on slower weekdays — without killing margins."
💡 Solution — Your Platform
"Our dineout platform helps you turn empty seats into paying guests by giving you visibility inside noon's app — and a simple way for people to discover, book, and pay here. Not on delivery."
🎁 The Offer
"For the first 60 days, we waive lead-gen fees entirely. In return, you run an exclusive launch offer of at least 10% for guests who come through us — focused on the exact time slots you want to fill."
🔒 Close
"If this structure feels fair, we can lock in your offer bands today and go live as one of the founding partners of the hall — before opening."
Drill tip: Record yourself 3–5 times. Aim to sound calm, not salesy. Each block should flow naturally into the next.
05

Objection Playbook

Rule: Pause → Acknowledge → Validate → Redirect to value. Never be defensive. Objections = they're seriously considering it.
😤
"Discounts kill our margins."
+
Acknowledge: "Totally fair — and that's exactly why we've structured this carefully."

Redirect: "We limit the offer to specific days, specific times, and app-paid bills only — a 2-month controlled test. Our aim is to monetize seats that would otherwise be completely empty, not discount business you'd already get. We'd review data together at week 4 and adjust or stop if it's not working."

Math: Share the example — AED 900/month to acquire 15 brand-new customers.
🔄
"We're already on Zomato / Talabat / Entertainer."
+
Validate: "That's perfect — it means you already believe in digital demand. We're not here to replace any of those."

Differentiate: "Those platforms mainly rotate the same discount-seeking customers. Noon gives you access to a completely different customer pool — shoppers, grocery buyers, daily app users — many of whom are NOT actively browsing dine-out apps at all. We don't cannibalize your business, we add net-new customers to it."

Angle: "Our focus is specifically dine-in and the food-hall experience — this launch offer is about owning that first wave of dine-in discovery."
💸
"We don't want to give big discounts."
+
Align: "Neither do we — let's work on an offer that attracts new customers while protecting your margins."

Offer lever: "Noon also funds 5% cashback for loyalty customers — so we're co-investing. Your side of the offer can be minimal."

If still resistant: "Fair enough — why don't we start on a 2-month trial of 0% commission, put up a minimal offer, and make an informed decision with zero commitment?"
📉
"Can we do less than 10%?"
+
Anchor high first: "Partners that see real traction usually sit around 15–20% on selected slots — that's what actually gets customer attention and drives volume."

Deliberate concession: "If we need to protect margins, we can structure 10% always-on, plus occasional 15–20% bursts for events or your slowest periods."

Reciprocity: When they accept 10–15%, you give something small back — priority listing, extra social posts, more frequent reporting.
📅
"We don't need more customers on weekends — we're already full."
+
Validate: "Perfect — that's actually great news. It means the offer is already working on weekends."

Redirect: "We can set the offer to activate only on weekdays, or during specific hours when you have the most empty seats. You keep full control of when and where the offer appears."
😞
"We tried these platforms before — they didn't work."
+
Empathize: "That's a legitimate concern — and honestly, most platforms fail because they're discount-led, not demand-led. They just rotate bargain hunters."

Differentiate: "Noon brings high-intent shoppers already spending inside our ecosystem and converts them into diners. That's why partners see return visits, not just one-time deal chasers."

De-risk: "That's exactly why we offer the 60-day zero-commitment trial — so you can see the numbers before you decide anything."
🎨
"We don't want to lose control of our brand."
+
Reassure immediately: "You keep full control — pricing, menu, availability, offers, and timings are all managed by you. Noon only brings you the customer. You decide how you want to serve them."
⏰
"We're too busy to manage another platform."
+
Remove the friction: "Setup is handled entirely by us — you don't touch a thing. Once you're live, most partners spend less than 5 minutes a week managing their noon profile."
🔒 Universal Close Line
"Based on everything we discussed, are you comfortable going ahead with the 2-month launch plan — with at least 10% for customers through us on the slots we agreed?"
06

Psychology Tactics That Work

⚡
Commitment & Consistency
Ask discovery questions first. When they verbalize their problems (dead hours, low weekday traffic), they've committed those as real — making your solution feel logical, not sales-y.
🛡️
Loss Aversion
Restaurants fear making a bad decision more than they want gains. Frame the 60-day trial as a "zero-risk test" — the risk of inaction (empty seats) is bigger than the risk of trying.
👥
Social Proof
Reference similar restaurants and the area. "Brands like yours see best traction with..." Even general platform stats help. Position as: everyone serious is already doing this.
⏳
Scarcity & Anchoring
"Founding partner" status is limited — early movers get the best app placement and marketing priority. Create urgency around the launch window without being pushy.
🎁
Reciprocity
Noon waives fees first (your concession). When they give something (accept 10–15%), reciprocate with something small: priority listing, more social posts, extra reporting. Makes them feel the deal is fair.
🪞
Mirroring & Labelling
Reflect back what they say: "So if I heard you right, weekdays are the concern and margins need protecting?" This shows you listened, lowers their guard, and builds alignment before the pitch.
🧲
Reframe Objections
Treat objections as buying signals, not rejection. They're asking you to resolve specific fears. Pause → Empathize → Diagnose the real fear → Answer that, not the surface complaint.
🔑
Give Them Control
Always present 2–3 offer options and let them choose. "You're in control — you pick the time slots, the discount depth, the menu items." Control reduces resistance dramatically.
07

Body Language & Delivery

🪑
Posture
Sit upright with a slight forward lean. Shoulders relaxed. Feet flat on floor. Never cross your arms — it signals closed off. Open posture = open mind.
👐
Hands
Keep hands visible on the table. Use calm, open gestures for key moments ("2 months", "10%", "founding partners"). Avoid fidgeting or pointing.
👁️
Eye Contact
When stating the offer, hold eye contact for 1–2 seconds, then glance at your one-pager. Makes it feel collaborative, not confrontational. Don't stare them down.
🎭
Mirroring Energy
Match their pace. If they're calm and slow — don't go hyper. If they're direct — keep answers tight. Mirroring builds unconscious rapport fast.
⏸️
The Pause
After making the offer — pause. Silence is powerful. Resist the urge to fill it. Let them think. The first person to speak after an offer often concedes.
😌
Calm Under Pressure
When they object, don't tense up. Take a breath. A slight nod before responding signals: "I hear you, I'm not rattled." Confidence is contagious.

Pre-Meeting Checklist

Check these off before every meeting:
✓
Know the restaurant's name, their brands in the hall, and any public info on their vibe/menu
✓
Prepare a compliment or observation about each brand to open with
✓
Know their likely dead hours (e.g. weekday lunch, late evenings) before walking in
✓
Have your one-pager ready: 60-day fee waiver, offer options, expected benefits, "founding partner" angle
✓
Have the math example ready (AED 200 avg bill × 15 customers = AED 900/month in offers for new diners)
✓
Rehearse the 60-second pitch out loud at least 3 times
✓
Role-play the top 3 objections (margins, existing platforms, less than 10%) and your responses
✓
Have your 4 discovery questions memorized or on your phone
✓
Know what "founding partner" benefits you can offer (priority listing, social mentions, reporting)
✓
Know the post-trial commercials: 3% lead gen + 2% PG, no signup fee, no subscription
✓
Have a clear close line ready and a minimum acceptable outcome (sign today vs. follow-up call)
✓
Do a 2-min mirror check — posture, open hands, calm expression
